## 4. Narrative

This report concerns customer C00348, card C00348-K1, issued as a mastercard debit.[^1] Between 2017-01-07 and 2017-01-28, 6 transaction(s) totalling $23.17 were identified as part of one fraud episode, beginning with transaction 3660245.[^2] The activity took place online, product code(s) C, billing region(s) 264, 325.[^3] The transactions were small online charges of a few dollars spread over several weeks, never followed by a larger purchase, the same shape appearing on other customers' cards in the same weeks.[^4] The activity is assessed as an undocumented coordinated pattern: The slow micro-charge shape appears on 11 other customers' cards with no micro-charge habit in the 30 days to 2017-01-28 against 1.0 per 30 days over the previous 3 windows: charges too small to trip any threshold, repeated across customers; 6 online charges of $2.10 to $5.08 within 21.2 days on card C00348-K1, each under $10, with no larger purchase after them and only 0 such charge in the 90 days before: slow card probing rather than the one-hour R5 test.[^5] The shared element (the same slow micro-charge shape (5 or more online charges under $6 in 30 days on a card with no micro-charge habit)) links this activity to 11 other card(s), including C00129-K1, C01326-K2, C01525-K2, C02851-K2, which indicates a common actor across cardholders.[^6] Earlier confirmed cases CC-2971, CC-3035, CC-2985 show the same pattern.[^7] The cardholder reported the disputed transaction to the bank and denied making it.[^8] The bank has recommended BLOCK_CARD, MONITOR_CONNECTED_CARDS and opened an internal case; the report is filed under R9, §3a.[^9]
