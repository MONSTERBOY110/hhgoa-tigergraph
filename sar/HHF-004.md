## 4. Narrative

This report concerns customer C12975, card C12975-K2, issued as a visa debit.[^1] Between 2017-01-05 and 2017-01-26, 6 transaction(s) totalling $23.99 were identified as part of one fraud episode, beginning with transaction 3660255.[^2] The activity took place online, product code(s) C, billing region(s) 191, 337, from device profile(s) rv:11.0 | | ie 11.0 for desktop | .[^3] The transactions were small online charges of a few dollars spread over several weeks, never followed by a larger purchase, the same shape appearing on other customers' cards in the same weeks.[^4] The activity is assessed as an undocumented coordinated pattern: The slow micro-charge shape appears on 7 other customers' cards with no micro-charge habit in the 30 days to 2017-01-26 against 1.0 per 30 days over the previous 3 windows: charges too small to trip any threshold, repeated across customers; Card C12975-K2 already has 1 confirmed-fraud closed case. The account key is not usable in January, so the precedent is read at card level, where it is weaker: on cards of this size an earlier confirmed case made a new alert about 3.9 times as likely to be fraud.[^5] The shared element (the same slow micro-charge shape (5 or more online charges under $6 in 30 days on a card with no micro-charge habit)) links this activity to 7 other card(s), including C00348-K1, C01525-K2, C02851-K2, C05083-K1, which indicates a common actor across cardholders.[^6] Earlier confirmed cases CC-4890, CC-2971, CC-3035 show the same pattern.[^7] The cardholder reported the disputed transaction to the bank and denied making it.[^8] The bank has recommended BLOCK_CARD, MONITOR_CONNECTED_CARDS and opened an internal case; the report is filed under R9, §3a.[^9]
