This narrative pertains to unauthorized credit card activity identified under case HHF-002 involving customer C06220 and visa credit card C06220-K1.[^1] Between January 17, 2017, and January 19, 2017, an unauthorized party conducted three fraudulent online transactions totaling $2,166.71 on the customer's card.[^2] The first transaction, transaction 3660125, occurred on 2017-01-17 at 19:58 for $637.13 in billing region 330 using device SM-J700T1 | iOS 11.1.2 | mobile safari 11.0 | 2048x1536 under product code C.[^3] The second transaction, transaction 3660126, took place on 2017-01-18 at 19:48 for $758.80 in billing region 384 using device SM-J700T1 | iOS 11.1.1 | mobile safari 11.0 | 2048x1536 under product code C.[^4] The final flagged transaction, transaction 3660127, was processed on 2017-01-19 at 18:18:58 for $770.78 in billing region 126 from device profile SM-J700T1 | | | under product code C.[^5] These transactions were executed through bursts of online purchases from devices new to the account operating behind an anonymous proxy.[^6] When contacted, the cardholder stated they did not make the transaction(s), did not share the card details, and still has the card.[^7] The identified activity is suspicious because the observed pattern matches none of the documented fraud typologies.[^8] Furthermore, this identical template of product C purchases of $200 or more in bursts from new devices behind an anonymous proxy appeared across 14 other cards within 14 days, including cards C00450-K1, C02342-K1, C03528-K1, C05061-K1, C05704-K2, C05770-K1, C06962-K2, and C07212-K2.[^9] Notably, none of these connected cards shared a physical device with card C06220-K1, indicating coordinated abuse across multiple customer accounts.[^10]
