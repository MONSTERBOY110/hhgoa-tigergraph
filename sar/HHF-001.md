## 4. Narrative

This report concerns customer C05061, card C05061-K1, issued as a visa credit.[^1] Between 2017-01-12 and 2017-01-14, 3 transaction(s) totalling $2,189.02 were identified as part of one fraud episode, beginning with transaction 3660106.[^2] The activity took place online, product code(s) C, billing region(s) 264, 492, from device profile(s) RCT6303W87M7 Build/MRA58K | Windows 10 | ie 11.0 for tablet | 1920x1080; RCT6303W87M7 Build/MRA58K | Windows 7 | ie 11.0 for desktop | .[^3] The transactions were bursts of online purchases from devices new to the account behind an anonymous proxy, the same template appearing on other customers' cards with no device in common.[^4] The activity is assessed as an undocumented coordinated pattern: The same template appears on 14 other cards within 14 days, none sharing a device with this card: coordinated abuse across customers; Card C05061-K1 already has 3 confirmed-fraud closed case. The account key is not usable in January, so the precedent is read at card level, where it is weaker: on cards of this size an earlier confirmed case made a new alert about 3.9 times as likely to be fraud.[^5] The shared element (a repeated template of product C purchases of $200 or more in bursts from devices marked New behind an anonymous proxy, with no shared device) links this activity to 14 other card(s), including C00450-K1, C02342-K1, C03528-K1, C05704-K2, which indicates a common actor across cardholders.[^6] Earlier confirmed cases CC-5229, CC-2674, CC-2420 show the same pattern.[^7] The cardholder has not yet been contacted.[^8] The bank has recommended BLOCK_CARD, MONITOR_CONNECTED_CARDS and opened an internal case; the report is filed under R9, §3a.[^9]
